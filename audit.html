The Founder's Executive Function Audit

Pick a founder task from this week that didn't get done. Now sit with this: is it stalled because it's hard — or because your brain has nothing to work with? This audit maps your executive function patterns so you can stop calling yourself lazy and start building around how you actually work.

Question 1 of 7 0%
Question 01

When a task on your to-do list keeps getting pushed to tomorrow, what's usually the real reason?

Be honest — not what you tell your co-founder.

A
There's no real deadline, so my brain treats it like it doesn't exist yet
B
It's boring — I've done this kind of task a hundred times and there's nothing new about it
C
I know it needs to happen but I genuinely don't know what the first step is
D
Nobody's going to notice if I do it — there's no visible payoff
Question 02

You have three hours blocked for deep work. What actually happens?

The calendar says "focus time." Your brain says...

A
I end up firefighting something urgent instead — the block never stood a chance
B
I start researching something tangentially related and go down a rabbit hole for two hours
C
I stare at the task, open six tabs, rearrange my desk, and accomplish nothing
D
I do the work but switch to something more stimulating halfway through
Question 03

When you're absolutely crushing it — in flow, doing your best work — what's usually true?

Think about the last time you were unstoppable.

A
There's a hard deadline — a launch, a board meeting, a demo in 48 hours
B
It's a brand new problem I've never solved before — my brain is obsessed with figuring it out
C
I know exactly what needs to happen — the steps are clear and I can just execute
D
Someone I respect is going to see the result — the stakes feel personal
Question 04

Your investor asks for a monthly update. It's been three months. What happened?

No judgment. Just pattern recognition.

A
There's no consequence for not sending it, so it never becomes "real" enough to do
B
Writing the same format update every month makes me want to claw my eyes out
C
I don't know what to say — I overthink what to include and the draft never feels ready
D
It feels performative — I'm building, not writing recaps nobody reads
Question 05

You need to hire someone. The job post has been half-written for two weeks. Why?

The team needs help. Your brain needs something else.

A
It's not on fire yet — I can still do everything myself (barely)
B
Writing job descriptions is the most boring task on the planet
C
I keep second-guessing the role — should it be full-time? Part-time? A contractor? I can't decide
D
Nobody's going to thank me for a job post — I'd rather spend that energy on the product
Question 06

Be honest: when do you actually reply to important emails?

Not when you read them. When you reply.

A
When someone follows up or I realize it's about to become a problem
B
Only the interesting ones — partnership offers, press, anything unexpected
C
When I finally figure out what to say — some emails sit in my head for days
D
When I know the reply will move something forward — otherwise it feels pointless
Question 07

A task you've been avoiding for weeks suddenly gets done in 20 minutes. What changed?

Something flipped the switch. What was it?

A
The deadline became real — someone was waiting on it TODAY
B
I found a new way to do it — a different tool, a different angle, something fresh
C
Someone told me exactly what to do and the ambiguity disappeared
D
I realized someone would actually see and appreciate the output
